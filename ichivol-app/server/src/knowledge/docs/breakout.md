# Breakout

Beginner

Updated Jul 29, 2026

## What Is a Breakout?

A breakout is when an asset’s price moves above a resistance area or below a support area, often signaling a potential new trend.

In [technical analysis (TA)](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/articles/what-is-technical-analysis), a breakout above a [resistance](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/glossary/resistance) level or below a [support](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/glossary/support) level suggests that price may begin trending in the direction of the move. A breakout can act as either a buy or a sell signal, depending on whether price breaks upward or downward.

## How Breakout Trading Works

Breakouts often follow a period of reduced [volatility](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/glossary/volatility), where price consolidates within a range or chart pattern such as a triangle, flag, wedge, or head and shoulders. Generally, the longer a pattern develops, the more significant the resulting move may be.

When clearly defined levels break, many traders may place their [stop-loss](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/articles/what-is-a-stop-limit-order) orders around the same price. As those orders trigger, they can create a cascading effect that accelerates the move. The core idea is simple: if support and resistance keep an asset’s price contained in a range, breaking that range may indicate a larger move ahead. Breakout trading can be applied across virtually any timeframe and trading style.

Breakouts accompanied by high volume are often considered stronger signals than those on average volume. When many participants act on the same level, the probability that price continues in the breakout direction may increase, though this is never guaranteed.

## False Breakouts and Confirmation

Like other TA indicators, breakouts can be subjective, since different traders may interpret support, resistance, and chart patterns differently. When price breaks a level but quickly reverses back into the range, the move is often called a false breakout, or fakeout.

To reduce the risk of acting on a fakeout, some traders wait for confirmation before entering. Common confirmation signals include a candle closing beyond the level rather than a brief wick, a rise in trading volume compared to the prior range, and a successful retest where the broken level holds as new support or resistance. A breakout on shrinking volume is often viewed as a weaker, less reliable signal, and confirming a move on a higher timeframe can add further context.
