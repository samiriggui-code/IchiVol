# Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) Explained

Intermediate

Updated Jun 12, 2026

## Key Takeaways

- VWAP stands for volume-weighted average price, a [technical analysis](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/articles/what-is-technical-analysis) indicator that combines price and trading volume into a single line.

- Traders may use the VWAP line to identify whether an asset is potentially trading above or below its average price for the day.

- VWAP is most useful as an intraday indicator and can lose accuracy when applied across multiple trading sessions.

- Institutional traders sometimes use VWAP to benchmark order execution and manage the market impact of large trades.

- Like all indicators, VWAP has limitations and works best when combined with other tools and a clear strategy.

## Introduction

Trading indicators help market participants analyze price movements and volume. Some indicators measure momentum, such as the [Relative Strength Index (RSI)](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/articles/what-is-the-rsi-indicator) or the [MACD](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/articles/macd-indicator-explained). Others help identify price levels and trends. The volume-weighted average price (VWAP) sits in a unique category: it combines both price and volume into one practical metric.

Volume is often considered one of the most important signals in market analysis. When you weight price by volume, you get a more accurate picture of where an asset has actually been trading, not just what its price was at a given moment.

This article explains what the VWAP is, how it is calculated, what it can tell traders, and where its limitations lie.

## What Is the VWAP?

VWAP stands for volume-weighted average price. As the name suggests, it is the average price of an asset over a given period, adjusted for trading volume. A transaction with higher volume carries more weight in the calculation than a transaction with lower volume.

This makes the VWAP a useful benchmark. It can give an indication of the dominant market trend, as well as important areas of [liquidity](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/articles/liquidity-explained). Many traders and analysts use it alongside other indicators to get a clearer view of market conditions.

VWAP is displayed as a single line on a price chart, similar in appearance to a moving average. However, unlike a simple moving average, it accounts for volume at each price level, which many traders consider a more meaningful measure.

## How to Calculate VWAP

### The formula

Most trading platforms calculate VWAP automatically. Still, understanding the formula helps you use the indicator more effectively.

**VWAP = Sum of (Typical Price x Volume) / Sum of Volume**

The typical price for each [candlestick](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/articles/a-beginners-guide-to-candlestick-charts) period is calculated as: (High + Low + Close) / 3. You multiply this by the volume for that period to get the traded value. Dividing the cumulative traded value by the cumulative volume gives you the VWAP.

### A step-by-step example

Consider a five-minute VWAP. For the first five-minute candle, calculate the typical price and multiply it by the volume for that period. This gives you the first data point (n1).

For each successive candle, add the new traded value (n2, n3, and so on) to the running total and divide by the total volume up to that point. This cumulative nature is why the VWAP is sometimes called a cumulative indicator.

The longer the session runs, the more data points accumulate. A 200-minute VWAP will react more slowly to recent price changes than a 20-minute VWAP, since older data has more influence.

## What the VWAP Tells Traders

### Trend and price context

When the price is above the VWAP line, some traders interpret this as a potentially bullish signal. When the price is below it, they may see it as potentially bearish. These interpretations always depend on the broader context of the chart and should not be used in isolation.

Some traders use the VWAP similarly to [moving averages](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/articles/moving-averages-explained). If the price crosses above the VWAP, they may consider a long position. If it crosses below, they may consider a short position. This is just one possible approach, and it carries its own risks.

A simple strategy some traders apply is to look for assets trading below their VWAP as potentially undervalued on a given day. However, in a strong uptrend, the price may stay above the VWAP for extended periods, making this signal unreliable on its own.

### Institutional use and order execution

VWAP is widely used by institutional traders and algorithmic trading systems. For large market participants, executing a big order all at once can move the market significantly. VWAP serves as a benchmark to measure how efficiently a trade was executed.

Buy orders filled below the VWAP are generally considered good fills, since they were executed below the average market price. Sell orders completed above the VWAP are similarly considered favorable. This benchmark helps institutions evaluate their trading desks and algorithms.

In crypto markets, VWAP-based algorithms have become increasingly common. Automated systems use VWAP to spread large orders across a trading session, reducing slippage and market impact.

## VWAP Limitations

VWAP works best for intraday analysis, meaning analysis within a single trading day. Applying it across multiple days can distort the average, since the cumulative calculation carries the influence of older sessions. For longer-term analysis, other tools are generally more appropriate. For more on intraday approaches, see the Academy guide to [day trading](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/articles/a-beginners-guide-to-day-trading-cryptocurrency).

Like moving averages, VWAP is a lagging indicator. It is calculated from past price and volume data, so it cannot predict future price movements. Traders should keep this in mind when using it to identify entry or exit points.

Another limitation is that VWAP can be slow to react in fast-moving markets. During periods of high volatility, prices can move far from the VWAP line before the indicator catches up. Relying on VWAP alone in such conditions may result in missed opportunities or poorly timed trades.

No indicator works perfectly in all market conditions. VWAP is most effective when used as one tool within a broader strategy that includes solid understanding of market context.

## FAQ

### What does VWAP stand for?

VWAP stands for volume-weighted average price. It is a technical indicator that calculates the average price of an asset over a given period, with each price point weighted by the trading volume at that level.

### Is VWAP useful for crypto trading?

Yes, many crypto traders use VWAP as part of their analysis. It can help identify potential trend direction and key price levels. Algorithmic trading systems also commonly use VWAP as an execution benchmark to reduce market impact when filling large orders.

### What does it mean when price is above or below VWAP?

When the price is above the VWAP, some traders consider the market potentially bullish for that session. When the price is below the VWAP, some may see it as potentially bearish. These signals are not definitive and should always be read in context with other indicators and price action.

### Can you use VWAP for long-term analysis?

VWAP is generally best suited to intraday analysis. Applying it across multiple days introduces distortion because the cumulative average carries the weight of older sessions. For longer-term analysis, indicators like moving averages or trend lines are typically more appropriate.

### How is VWAP different from a moving average?

Both VWAP and moving averages display a smoothed price line on a chart. The key difference is that VWAP weights each price point by volume, while a simple moving average treats all periods equally. This means VWAP reflects actual trading activity more directly, which is why many institutional traders prefer it as a benchmark.

## Closing Thoughts

The VWAP is a straightforward but powerful indicator. By combining price and volume data, it gives traders a view of the average price at which an asset has actually been traded over a session, not just where it happened to be at a given moment.

Traders may use VWAP to assess trend direction, identify potential entry and exit levels, or benchmark order execution. Institutional and algorithmic traders rely on it heavily for managing large positions efficiently.

That said, VWAP is a lagging indicator with no predictive power. It works best when used alongside other tools and a clear plan for [risk management](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/articles/a-beginners-guide-to-understanding-risk-management). Used thoughtfully, it can be a valuable addition to a trader's toolkit.
